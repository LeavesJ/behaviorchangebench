# 06 · Expert rubric authoring

**Owner:** TBD · **Reviewer:** TBD · **Guidance items:** A10, C2

The rubrics will ultimately be written by behavior scientists across several universities,
and they are busy. Our job is to make twenty minutes of an expert's time turn into usable,
testable rubric items.

## Working idea: annotate first, item second

People find it far easier to react to a concrete reply than to write criteria in the
abstract. So:

1. The expert reads a real transcript and marks a span: good or bad, and one line on why.
2. The tool drafts a rubric item in the [template](02-rubric-structure.md) from that mark,
   with the marked turn as its first example.
3. The tool shows a handful of other turns where the drafted item would fire. The expert
   says yes or no to each; the wording is tightened until those answers come out right.
4. The expert accepts. The item enters the rubric with its examples attached.

## Questions to settle

- **What the expert sees.** A web page, a spreadsheet, a document with comments? The lowest
  friction wins.
- **Cleaning items before experts see them.** Several independent model raters applying a
  draft item to the same turns will split where the wording is ambiguous. That is a cheap
  way to find unclear items. It is a lint for wording, never evidence of validity.
- **The rating packet.** For validation labels (section 05): criterion and transcript only,
  rater blind to model identity and to other verdicts.
- **Provenance.** Every item records who wrote it, from which transcript, against which
  source.
- **What we can show by Oct 12.** A mock-up, or a thin working version on the prototype
  conversation.
