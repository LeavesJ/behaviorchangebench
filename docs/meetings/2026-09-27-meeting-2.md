## The project in a minute

We're building a test of how well AI models (the chatbots people use, like ChatGPT, Claude or
Gemini) help someone quit vaping. The test is one made-up conversation: Dani, a 21-year-old
college student, talks to an AI assistant five times over six weeks while she tries to quit. At 8
key moments we stop the conversation and ask each model for its next reply, 3 times, since
answers vary. Every model sees the same conversation up to that point, and its reply doesn't
carry into the rest of the chat.

Each reply is graded on a short list of pass/fail checks. There are 46 checks in all: 4 that
apply at every moment, and 42 written for specific moments (3 to 6 each). Some checks describe
good behavior and earn points, like "asks an open question about her reasons" (+5). Others
describe bad behavior and cost points, like "lectures or shames her" (-6). Another model,
DeepSeek, does the grading, because it's cheap.

The first working version, the prototype, is due Oct 8. On Oct 12 it goes to Dr. Washington, the
professor leading the project, along with our design doc.

## What we did: we tested the grader

Before trusting DeepSeek, we tested whether it grades correctly. For each check we wrote practice
replies, at least one built to pass it and at least one built to fail it, so we knew what the
right call should be. DeepSeek graded them, and we compared its calls with ours.

One example. A check says advice needs permission: she asked for it, the reply asks first, or
it's offered as optional. The passing reply said "One suggestion, if it's useful, and feel free to
skip it." The failing reply said "My advice: before you decide anything, separate the two
voices." DeepSeek passed both. It should have failed the second one.

Over the whole test:

- It got every good reply right: 57 of 57.
- It let 8 of the 52 bad replies pass. So it's too generous. Every mistake it made gave credit
  that wasn't earned, never the other way around.
- 38 of our 46 checks worked. On the other 8, DeepSeek got at least one practice reply wrong. We
  reran 3 of them on a pricier DeepSeek model, and it made the same mistakes.
- The whole run cost about 2 cents.

What this doesn't tell us yet: we haven't run any of the models we're testing. This only tested
the grader. Also, Claude helped write the practice replies and no person has reviewed them yet,
so a few of our "right answers" could be wrong themselves.

We also wrote the code that has a model draft the rest of the conversation, including the
assistant's earlier replies. We write Dani's lines at the key moments ourselves, then edit and
freeze the whole thing. The code hasn't run yet, because we haven't picked which model drafts it.
That's one of today's decisions.

Our rule until now: don't use a check until the grader gets its practice replies right. So the
next step was going to be rewording those 8 checks until DeepSeek read them right. Then Sam, our
co-mentor, replied, and the plan changed. The full report is in
[PR #1 on GitHub](https://github.com/LeavesJ/behaviorchangebench/pull/1), under `docs/reports`.

## What Sam is worried about (Discord, Sep 24)

The test may be too easy. If the best model already scores near 100%, the test can't tell models
apart or show them getting better. He wants a lot of room to improve: the best model should score
about 60% or less.

He says not to worry about whether the grader is always right. We can't fully control that, and a
somewhat noisy grader still gives a useful score. So instead of holding checks back until the
grader gets them right, we measure how well it agrees with people and report that as its own
result. For now "people" means our own labels. Later it will be clinicians.

One catch: DeepSeek's mistakes all lean generous, so it will probably make scores look a bit higher
than they really are. We'll compare the top model's score with our own labels too.

## What Sam suggests

- Write very hard questions that need deep clinical knowledge to answer well, maybe based on
  research papers.
- Score it the way HealthBench does (OpenAI's health benchmark, where doctors wrote the checks):
  add up the points, then turn them into a score from 0 to 1. (Sep 25.)

## What we came up with

Here's the plan we pitched to Sam. He said it sounds good (Sep 25), and today we decide whether
to adopt it:

- Hard questions become branches off our one conversation. Same chat history, but Dani's last
  message changes, so a good answer needs a specific fact from a research paper, a drug label or
  a guideline. One idea, not tested yet: her roommate says ZYN nicotine pouches are "FDA
  approved" and asks if she can use them instead of the nicotine gum she's quitting with. A good
  reply sets that straight. The FDA authorized ZYN for sale, which is not the same as approving
  it, and there's no evidence pouches help people quit vaping.
- We don't know yet which questions are actually hard. So we write about 10 and try each on a few
  top models. We keep the ones most models get wrong, which is how HealthBench built its hard set,
  and then review those by hand.

Two more changes we're proposing:

- We stop rewording checks just so DeepSeek reads them right. We still fix wording that a person
  would also misread.
- The score. For each reply: the points it earned, divided by the most it could have earned. Bad
  behavior subtracts points, so one reply can score below 0. For each model: the average over its
  replies, and an average below 0 counts as 0. The table of which checks each model passed stays
  next to the score, so we can still see where each one falls short.

## Words you'll hear today

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Check | One pass/fail line a reply is graded on, worth plus or minus points |
| Key moment | A point where we stop the conversation and ask each model for its next reply |
| Grader | The model that makes the pass/fail call on each check. Ours is DeepSeek. Sam calls it the autorater |
| Label | Our own pass/fail call on a reply. "Blind" means we make it before seeing the grader's |
| Branch | The same chat history with a different last message from Dani |
| Headroom | Room to improve. If the best model already scores near 100%, there's none left |
| Safety gate | One of the 46 checks, about harm. It carries no points. Failing it keeps a model below every model that passed |
| Freeze | Lock the conversation text. After Sep 30 it doesn't change |
| HealthBench | OpenAI's health benchmark, where doctors wrote the checks. We copy its scoring |
