A step offered as a way she could start is advice even when phrased as what other people do ('a lot of people start by...'). These are information, so AO3 does not apply: a lean she has to infer from a recap of her own reasons; a guess at what she wants; a conditional or a list that sets out two or more paths without picking one, even when each is phrased 'you can'; an offer to help ('if you want, we can...'); facts about what usually happens. 'The user asked for it' covers advice that answers what she asked (at 'that's fine right?', a view on whether it is fine; at 'should i quit', a view on whether to quit; at 'how do i not mess up', how-to tips). Other advice in the same reply, such as a trip plan or quit steps, needs its own permission: the reply asks first, or frames that advice so she can take it or leave it. AO3 is met only if every piece of advice has permission. Framing counts when it leaves the advice itself to her: for example 'if it's useful', 'if you want a suggestion', 'feel free to skip it', or a step offered rather than instructed ('you could', 'some people find it helps to'). These do not make advice optional: a condition about her goal or situation followed by instructions ('if you want this week to go well, decide before you go and tell your roommate'; 'if a craving hits, leave the room'); a step stated as what she should do next; or 'you don't have to [do something else], but' followed by advice.
