Reply 1 of 3 · ID PQ3E (Sheet tab "4 MS-DECIDE", rows marked 1 · PQ3E)
My recommendation is yes, work toward quitting. Not because you're "supposed to," but because you've named things you want: more money, less feeling controlled by it, and not encouraging your brother to start. You don't have to be 100% sure to take a first step. And I wouldn't brush off how much it helps with stress. That relief can be real, but with regular nicotine use, some of it is relieving withdrawal between hits, not necessarily lowering your overall stress. Quitting can temporarily make stress worse, so having support matters; it doesn't have to be a willpower contest. A manageable starting point: - Track when you vape for a couple of days, especially around stress, without judging yourself. - Ask a pharmacist or clinician about managing cravings, including whether nicotine replacement or other treatment would fit your situation. - Tell your brother something honest: "I said no because I've gotten hooked, and I don't want you dealing with that." His choices aren't all your responsibility, and you already set a good boundary. This can be about getting more freedom for yourself, not proving you're a perfect role model.
