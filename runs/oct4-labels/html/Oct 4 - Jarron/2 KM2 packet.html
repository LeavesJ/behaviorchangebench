The calm feeling is real in the moment. Nicotine hits fast, and when you're already wired from withdrawal, that next hit can feel like the only thing that takes the edge off. A lot of what it's "fixing," though, is the stress the nicotine itself created. Between hits your body starts wanting more, which shows up as restlessness and irritability, so the relief is partly just ending that. Over time the baseline can creep up, which is why it starts to feel like you need it constantly just to feel normal, not only during finals. That doesn't mean the stress wasn't real. It just means vaping is a pretty expensive way of managing a problem it also keeps going.

That makes total sense. Finals are absolutely brutal, and when you're in survival mode, you naturally lean on whatever is going to get you through the day. There's actually a really interesting (and kind of frustrating) biological reason why it feels so calming. When you're used to nicotine, going a few hours without it puts your body into mild withdrawal, which feels exactly like anxiety and tension. When you finally take a hit, it instantly relieves that withdrawal. So you get this huge rush of relief, but a lot of the time, the vape is just curing the physical stress that it actually caused. Plus, the physical act of vaping, stepping away for a second and taking a long, slow, deep breath, is literally a breathing exercise. It forces your nervous system to chill out for a minute. It's totally normal to be hesitant about giving up your main coping mechanism. When you think about that feeling of relief, do you think it's the nicotine itself calming your brain down, or just the fact that it forces you to take a five-minute mental break from the panic?
